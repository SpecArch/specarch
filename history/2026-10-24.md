# 2026-10-24

## Capability check for three jobs

The owner starts three jobs with SpecArch today and asked what it
lacks for each: a website from a design tool's file, an information
system signing in through single sign-on, and a multi-tenant office
suite built as an estate of modules. `docs/capability-check-2026-10-24.md`
is the answer: each job walked from the first requirement to a
deployed, tested system as an analyst, an architect and a tester would,
with what SpecArch specifies and generates today, what it cannot, and
what the owner does by hand; every gap named with its severity for the
job (blocks, workaround, nice to have), the smallest way to close it, the
release it would belong to by the version rule of `docs/maintenance.md`,
and the schema line, document line or code line it rests on; the gaps
ranked across the jobs by what the owner meets first; the assumptions
marked. No code, schema or design changed.

The conclusion, in short. For the website: no page kind for content, no
reader of a design file, a theme without typography or assets. For the
SSO system: no element for the sign-in flow through a provider, no
sign-out, no mapping from the provider's claims to roles; the tokens,
clients, audience, security events and their derived cases cover the
resource-server side already. For the office suite: the tenant scope is
one rows rule per role and per entity with an unlisted entity reached
whole, cross-module screens are refused as `reference`, and the editors
and anything live have no page kind and no interface beyond HTTP.
Across the jobs the first five gaps the owner meets are the tenant
scope, the content page, the provider sign-in flow, a page kind for a
hand-built screen, and generation across the estate.

The handoff mailbox was empty. Left out on purpose: which customer or
product asked for each job, and any name, data or detail of theirs; the
jobs are described in generic words only.

Review round 1 of the check asked for four changes, all made in the
document alone. The content page's proposal (G1.2) now adds `blocks` to
a page, since `sections` already means a form's or view's fields in
titled groups and one key must not carry two meanings. The site
metadata (G1.7) gets `metaDescription` and `canonical` of its own, since
a page's `description` is the design's prose and stays out of the
output. The conclusion says its order plainly, the most severe first
and then the order met, and its lists follow the ranking table. Job 3's
gaps are numbered G3.1 to G3.10 without a skip; the count stays 32.

## Closing the blocking gaps: the order, the items and the proof

The owner asked, after the capability check, for the gaps that block
one working model: the analyst and the designer write the specification,
an agent fills in what is left, generates the code, runs the tests and
takes the system to deployment, with nothing done by hand between.
`docs/closing-the-gaps-2026-10-24.md` answers in three parts. First the
filter, which is not the check's "blocks": a gap blocks the pipeline
when the specification cannot name the thing, a verb refuses it, or the
output is wrong by omission with nothing said; a hole a compiler, a
generator warning or a document names (handler bodies, the harness,
test bodies, the container recipe, the records) does not block, since
the agent fills it where it is named. Under that filter six gaps block,
in the order they close: the tenant scope said once (G3.1), the
generated dxlib service applying the rows rules (G3.1b, a finding of
this item), the content page (G1.2), the sign-in flow through an
identity provider (G2.1), a page kind for a screen built by hand
(G3.7), and generation across the estate (G3.4). The other 26 are
classed one by one with the reason. The deployable artifact and the
deployment record were checked as steps of their own: the build task and
the release and commissioning records cover them.

Second, six serial task items were queued, 1204 to 1209, one per gap in
that order, each saying what done looks like, the conformance cases
that prove it, the job it unblocks and the owner's question it waits on.
Every schema change is additive to meta-model 0.3; the decisions that
are the owner's (the tenant shorthand the meta-model deferred, OpenID
Connect first and additive only with SAML later, an interface beyond
HTTP, a generated container recipe, who approves in this model and
whether a draft build may go to a test environment) are questions in the
document, not taken.

Third, the proof: three scratch specifications, the smallest example of
each job, run through the toolchain built from this commit, with the
commands and the lines. The one-page site stops at the specification
(`content is not allowed here`); its contact form alone goes through to
generated screens. The login with single sign-on validates as a
resource server, generates and compiles against the local dxlib checkout
with one body named by the compiler, but a page cannot leave the
application and no red case of the flow is derived. The two tenants go
through `validate`, `gaps`, `document`, `approve` and `generate` after
the approval, compile with one body, and the repository's round trip
runs a generated service and client under dxlib; but the generated
service calls dxlib's paging list and sets neither the caller setting
the written policies read nor the database role they bind, and the
service's own connection is the owner, which row security does not
bind, so the generated system answers every tenant's rows with no
warning. That finding is item 1205.

No code, schema or design changed. The scratch specifications are not
committed. The handoff mailbox was empty. Left out on purpose: the
customers or products behind the jobs and any detail of theirs.
