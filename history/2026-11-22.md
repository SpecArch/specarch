## PDF books and the business requirements document

`specarch document pdf` writes four PDF books from a specification:
`brd.pdf` (business requirements), `requirements.pdf` (requirements
specification and traceability matrix), `technical.pdf` (technical
specification, deployment and operations guides) and `qa.pdf` (test plan
and the traceability matrix as its coverage matrix). `specarch document
brd` writes the business requirements document as `brd.md`, in the order
of the business requirements specification of ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148, and it
is the first book's content (ADR-248, SA-148).

Each book is made from the Markdown the document targets write, so it says
nothing the specification does not and keeps every Problem, Open
question, Draft and Open change request paragraph at its entry. A book
opens with front matter (title, version, date, status, owners, what it was
made from, the change history from the release records, newest first),
then a table of contents with page numbers, the documents, and a Problems
and gaps list linking each mark both ways. The date is the approval's,
else the release record's of the version, else the book says the version
is not dated and marks it as a gap. Nothing comes from the clock, so the
same specification writes the same bytes, and `--check` and `approve`
compare the books like any document.

The PDF writer is SpecArch's own (`internal/pdf`): A4, tagged (structure
tree, reading order, language, artifacts for the header and footer),
real text in subsets of the Go fonts with a Unicode map, bookmarks, named
destinations, links from every element id to its heading or table row,
object streams and an xref stream. The fonts come from golang.org/x/image
v0.48.0 (BSD-3-Clause); `go get` also moved golang.org/x/sync to v0.25.0,
golang.org/x/sys to v0.50.0 and golang.org/x/text to v0.44.0. SBOM scan:
syft 1.57.1, grype 0.121.3, osv-scanner 2.7.1 and govulncheck 1.9.0 found
no vulnerability in a Go module. Over the committed tree grype names 8
Medium findings in `examples/library-lending/web-nextjs/pnpm-lock.yaml`
(next 16.4.1 and minimatch 10.2.6), a file this work does not touch;
run over a checkout it also names GO-2026-6859 in the Go standard library
of the binaries built under `.bin/` (fixed in 1.27.4) and gem findings
under `swift/.build/`, both ignored build output. The fonts' licence is in
`third_party/go-fonts/LICENSE` and NOTICE, and each book names the fonts
in its front matter.

Decided against, in ADR-248: a template or theme option (one layout keeps
the books comparable and `--check` exact), a `doc` command (`document`
already writes every document, and a PDF is one more target), rendering
from the specification directly (it would be a second writer of every
document that drifts from the Markdown), an HTML-to-PDF browser, other Go
PDF libraries (none writes tagged PDF; the ones that do are not under an
OSI licence) and drawing Mermaid (each diagram is printed as its source,
and the html document draws them).

New cases: document-brd, document-pdf-books (a valid specification dated
by its release record) and document-pdf-gaps (an invalid specification
with an open question and no date: exit 1, all four books written, every
gap marked and listed). 25 golden cases moved because the outputs table,
the capabilities text and the change register's reach now list `brd` and
`pdf`. `brd` is a target of SpecArch's own specification and of
examples/library-lending (`docs/brd.md` in each); adding it to
library-lending's implementation file moves the digest in the header of
its generated files. The pdf target is not configured in any example, so
no PDF is kept in the tree apart from the cases. `docs/pdf-documents.md`
describes the books; the README, both situation guides and the skill name
the two targets.

Nothing of an employer or client was involved; the examples are the
repository's own. The handoff mailbox was missing.

The work is in commits 34babb0e3 (the books, the brd document, the design,
the cases and the docs) and 02768350f (golang.org/x/sync v0.25.0 and
golang.org/x/sys v0.50.0, which `go get` moved, recorded in the libraries
of the Go implementation file).

After the first review: the table of contents ends with the Problems and
gaps list, so a reader of the printed book finds its page; the list puts
the front matter's marks under Front matter (Halaman depan in Indonesian),
since the front matter's first heading is the book's title, which the
requirements document's own heading repeats. document-unknown-target runs
`document no-such-target` again: with pdf a real target it had become a
second no-output-folder case. TestBookMarksInEveryLanguage writes the
books of document-pdf-gaps in every language and requires as many marks
listed as in English (7, 13, 12 and 11).
