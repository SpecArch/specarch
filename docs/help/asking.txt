Asking a person: one question at a time, with a recommended answer

The open questions are answered by people, and a person answers one
question with a choice better than a list. specarch gaps --next picks the
question to ask next; the agent puts it, with an answer it recommends;
specarch decide records the answer. Then the next one.

  specarch gaps --next <folder>

prints, per specification, the one open question ranked first: a must
question before a should, a should before a could; then the one holding
up more outputs (the documents whose sections it blocks, and code); then
the one blocking more entries; then the earlier stage; then the lower ID.
With it: who decides, what it blocks, what it holds up, its options, its
Insight and the decide command that records the answer. With --json the
same is under next, with recommendation null, for the agent to fill
before it passes the question on.

The procedure

1. Run specarch gaps --next. Ask the stakeholder it names under Decided
   by, a role; for material, under Needed from. When the person you are
   talking to is not that role, pass the question to the person who is.
2. Put that one question alone, in the words of its question line, with
   what it blocks in one sentence when the person needs it.
3. Give two to five options, numbered: the question's own when it has
   them, otherwise ones you write from the specification and its
   sources. Mark one recommended, with its reason in one line, and say
   how sure you are. The person may accept it in a word (yes, 2).
4. Record the answer with the decide line printed: the full text of the
   option chosen, never the word or the number the person said, and
   --source the interview it was given in. Run with --dry-run first when
   unsure what it writes.
5. Write the entries decide lists as blocked, from the answer, then
   specarch validate.
6. Run specarch gaps --next again. An answer can settle other questions
   or open new ones, so never ask a second question from the first
   ranking.

Stop when no must or should question is left, or when the person stops.
There is no fixed number of questions: a must question left unasked
leaves keys undefined, and gaps lists what is left. Never answer a
question yourself, and never pick between two sources that disagree; the
person does.

A material question asks for something to read: a document, a file, a
screenshot, a record. Ask for it the same way, one at a time; declare
what is received as a source of kind document and record it with decide
--source naming it.

When the person's answer is none of the options, record it in their
words. When they cannot answer, leave the question open and go to the
next one with gaps; say who else could answer it.

The program itself asks nothing: gaps --next prints and exits, and every
answer is recorded by decide, with its source and the entries it
settles. The order of questions, one at a time with a recommended option,
comes from Spec Kit's clarify command (github.com/github/spec-kit, MIT);
Spec Kit stops after five questions, SpecArch does not.
