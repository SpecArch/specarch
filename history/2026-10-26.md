## Design: state machines with control state, extended state, guards, updates and the rest

Asked by the owner (item 1285): state diagrams and state variables are
among the most important parts of a system architecture, and SpecArch
models only an entity's single control state and its moves. A design
item: `docs/state-machines.md` and four decision records with `status:
proposed`, ADR-189 to ADR-192, one requirement SA-98 with `status:
proposed`, and three sources (UML 2.5.1 clause 14, SCXML 1.0, Harel
1987); no code, schema or generator changes. The owner is asked to
approve or change the design before anything is built.

What it designs, each concept mapped to UML 2.5.1 and SCXML 1.0 with the
reason SpecArch follows or departs: a machine is a named element under
`stateMachines`, of an entity (`entity`, `stateField`) or standalone
(`stateEnum`, `variables`, `constraints`, `initial`); the control state
is always one enum's value and the extended state the entity's fields or
the variables; today's `stateField` and `transitions` stay the short form
of one flat machine, not deprecated (ADR-189). A transition has one
cause, `trigger` as today, `after` a duration or `at` a moment, a
condition `when` over the extended state and `event.<property>`,
`otherwise` for the remaining case, and `set` in the shape of
`writes.set`; two transitions of one state on one cause are an error
when they cannot be told apart and a warning when the validator cannot
show their conditions exclusive, never settled by document order
(ADR-190). A state carries `entry`, `exit`, `invariant`, child `states`
with `initial`, `history` and `final`; a stored machine's initial state is
its field's `default`; parallel states are several machines of one
entity, one field each, with `within` for one scoped to another's states;
no regions inside a state, no pseudostates, no do activity (ADR-191).
Ten rules in both builds, every one that can fire on a file written today
a warning; the diagram with causes, conditions and updates on the arrows,
variables as a note, nested states and parallel machines; the documents,
test plan and guarded script follow; the step is minor, 1.3.0 (ADR-192).

Decisions taken in the design, for the owner to confirm: the condition is
`when`, not `guard`, since `guard` already names an operation's checked
change and `when` a page action's condition; the short form is kept
rather than deprecated, since it is the full form with the machine's own
keys left out; the initial state of a stored machine comes from the
field's default and `initial` is refused on it; parallel states are
machines, not regions, since a record holds one value per field.

Checked with Mermaid before writing the diagram section: mermaid-cli
12.0.0 with the installed Chrome renders nested states two deep, the
concurrency separator, labels with brackets and slashes and multi-line
notes; a `state : text` line replaces the state's name with the text, so
entry and exit are notes; Mermaid has no history pseudostate, and an
arrow from outside to a state drawn inside a composite pulls that state
out of it, so history is a suffix on the arrow into the composite. The
OMG PDF of UML 2.5.1 could not be read by the tooling here; the clause
numbers cited (14.2.3.1, 14.2.3.4, 14.2.3.8, 14.2.3.9.3, 14.2.4) come
from the standard as remembered and from secondary sources, and the
owner's review may correct one.

The worked example is a generic job with the owner's `ProgressType` in
SpecArch's spelling (`not_started`, enum values are snake_case), a retry
counter against a maximum, a last error from the event, a composite
`running` with `warming` and `steady`, a pause resumed by history, a
deadline, a link machine `within` the running states, and a standalone
operator session; it is in the document, since the schema has no keys for
it yet. On this repository's specification and the examples the design
adds no line; three conformance cases with a state field would gain
warning lines when the rules are built. The design document is in
`docs/`, where this repository's design documents live, and the decision
records in `spec/`. Nothing of anyone's system is in it; nothing was left
out on purpose. The handoff mailbox was empty.

Open for the owner, in the document's section 10: `when` or `guard`; the
initial state from the default; the entry-time column for `after`; a
change event; an internal transition; a page event as a trigger; the
rule and key names before the release freezes them.

The owner approved the design as designed the same evening, so the four
records and SA-98 are accepted, and the build items follow in the order
of the document's section 11. The questions of section 10 stay open for
the build items to settle with the owner.

The review of round 1 found five places where the design contradicted
itself and two it left unsaid, corrected in the same files: the worked
example cleared `lastError` on entering `starting`, after the retry's
`set` had recorded the reason, so every retry lost it; now `running`
clears it on entry, which also establishes its invariant after a retried
start, and 3.4 says that an entry's `set` runs after the transition's.
`state_unused` excluded every initial state, and in the short form
without a default an unnamed enum value would have counted as a derived
initial state; now the derivation runs over the states the transitions
name, as the generators do today (the test plan's expected output draws
`[*] --> open` and never `lost`), so an unnamed value is `state_unused`
and never initial. `transition_ambiguous` was an error for two
transitions with neither `when` nor `otherwise`, which an operation that
may approve or reject writes today, against ADR-186; now that pair is a
warning, the pairs that need `when` or `otherwise` are errors, and
causeless transitions from a simple state are not grouped, since each
carries `transition_cause`. SA-98's first acceptance asked for no new
line on any file written today, which the warnings make impossible; it
asks for no new error on such a file and no new line on this repository's
specification and examples. The session example claimed two conditions
exclusive that the rule could not show, since one is a conjunction; the
rule gains the form in which an operand of a conjunction is exclusive
with the other condition, which is sound and is what a hand writes. One
clause number was cited two ways; 14.2.3.9.3 stands. A machine `within`
another was said to be drawn inside the composite that holds the states
it names, which `running` and `paused` do not share; it is drawn as a
region with a note naming the machine and the states, after the region's
block, since Mermaid refuses a note on a state it has not drawn yet (seen
with mermaid-cli 12.0.0). Section 8 now walks a start that fails twice
and succeeds through the rules, as the design's own check. Left out on
purpose, now named: the check the request called a transition missing
for a state the requirements name is `state_unused`, since the validator
does not read a requirement's prose and the link from a requirement to a
state is the enum value it asked for.
