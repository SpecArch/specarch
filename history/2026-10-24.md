## Capability check for three jobs

The owner starts three jobs with SpecArch today and asked what it
lacks for each: a website from a design tool's file, an information
system signing in through single sign-on, and a multi-tenant office
suite built as an estate of modules. `docs/capability-check-2026-10-24.md`
is the answer: each job walked from the first requirement to a
deployed, tested system as an analyst, an architect and a tester would,
with what SpecArch specifies and generates today, what it cannot, and
what the owner does by hand; every gap named with its severity for the
job (blocks, workaround, nice to have), the smallest way to close it, the
release it would belong to by the version rule of `docs/maintenance.md`,
and the schema line, document line or code line it rests on; the gaps
ranked across the jobs by what the owner meets first; the assumptions
marked. No code, schema or design changed.

The conclusion, in short. For the website: no page kind for content, no
reader of a design file, a theme without typography or assets. For the
SSO system: no element for the sign-in flow through a provider, no
sign-out, no mapping from the provider's claims to roles; the tokens,
clients, audience, security events and their derived cases cover the
resource-server side already. For the office suite: the tenant scope is
one rows rule per role and per entity with an unlisted entity reached
whole, cross-module screens are refused as `reference`, and the editors
and anything live have no page kind and no interface beyond HTTP.
Across the jobs the first five gaps the owner meets are the tenant
scope, the content page, the provider sign-in flow, a page kind for a
hand-built screen, and generation across the estate.

The handoff mailbox was empty. Left out on purpose: which customer or
product asked for each job, and any name, data or detail of theirs; the
jobs are described in generic words only.

Review round 1 of the check asked for four changes, all made in the
document alone. The content page's proposal (G1.2) now adds `blocks` to
a page, since `sections` already means a form's or view's fields in
titled groups and one key must not carry two meanings. The site
metadata (G1.7) gets `metaDescription` and `canonical` of its own, since
a page's `description` is the design's prose and stays out of the
output. The conclusion says its order plainly, the most severe first
and then the order met, and its lists follow the ranking table. Job 3's
gaps are numbered G3.1 to G3.10 without a skip; the count stays 32.

## The tenant scope said once

A specification can now say its tenant scope once, as `tenant` with the
caller's `property`, the `key` every tenant-owned entity carries and the
entities under `shared` that every tenant reads whole (ADR-179, SA-91).
This is the first of the six tasks of the closing-the-gaps plan (item
1204), and it waited on the owner's question Q2: meta-model 0.3 had left
the shorthand out until a specification was long enough to want it. The
owner answered yes: add it, additive to 0.3.

The loader derives the rules into the document as read, in both builds:
for every role and every entity with a field named `key` that `shared`
does not list, `roles.<role>.rows.<Entity>: <key> == caller.<property>`
at the position of `tenant`, unless the role writes its own rule for that
entity, which then stands alone. Since the rules are in the document,
`model`, the derived cases and `derive`'s drafts, the techspec's rows
table and `generate sql`'s row security policies all take them with no
code of their own; the techspec also gains a paragraph that names the
scope. The validator's new rule `tenant` refuses, at the entity, an
entity that carries no key, is not shared and is not limited by a rule of
its own on every role, so that an entity left out is never reached
whole; and a shared name that is no entity, a shared entity carrying the
key, a property the caller does not declare and a key the entity cannot
compare with the property. A derived rule is checked once, at the entity,
rather than at every role. An exception the item did not spell out was
taken on purpose: an entity without the key that every role limits with
its own rule is accepted, since an organisation's own record carries its
identifier as `id` and must be readable by its members only; listing it
as shared would open it to every tenant.

Nine conformance cases: validate-tenant-valid, validate-tenant-entity-without-key,
validate-tenant-rows-overridden, validate-tenant-property,
validate-tenant, model-tenant-rules, derive-tenant, generate-sql-tenant
and document-techspec-tenant. Two goldens that list the design stage's
sections gain `tenant`. `docs/conventions.md` and
`docs/meta-model-0.3.md` say the scope as the current truth, and the
latter's left-out table and `docs/dxlib-lessons.md` no longer call it
deferred. The
generated dxlib service applying the rules is the next task, not this
one. The handoff mailbox was empty. One deviation from the request was
taken on purpose and waits for the owner to confirm it: the request
refuses every entity that neither carries the key nor is listed under
shared, and the change accepts such an entity when every role writes its
own rule for it, so that an organisation's own record keyed by `id` can
be read by its members only (listing it as shared would open it to every
tenant). Nothing else was left out.

The review of round 1 found that the check failed open when the
specification had no roles: an entity that every role limits with a rule
of its own was taken as limited when there was no role at all, so an
entity without the key and not shared passed, and no rule was derived for
the entities that carry the key either. Both builds now refuse a scope in
a specification with no roles, once, at `tenant`, and stop there; ADR-179,
SA-91, the rule `tenant` and `docs/conventions.md` say so, and the new
case validate-tenant-no-roles shows it.

## The generated dxlib service applies the rows rules

Item 1205, second step. ADR-180 decides how the caller reaches the query
on dxlib: the handlers filter by the rule themselves, rather than each
request taking the database role and setting the caller's properties so
that the row security policies apply. The filter works on every engine
dxlib serves (generate sql writes policies on PostgreSQL only), gives a
caller holding several roles the union of their rows (one SET LOCAL ROLE
takes one role), and needs nothing dxlib lacks: the paging list takes a
builder the handler builds, and the builder binds named parameters. The
policies stay for any connection that takes a role and sets the
properties.

`generate go-dxlib` now declares `Caller` (`HasRole` and a method per
caller property, a pointer for a nullable one), which the service answers
from `callerOf`, written beside the file, and per entity with a rule
`rows<Entity>`, the rules of the roles the caller holds joined with OR,
none when a role they hold has no rule, `1 = 0` when they hold none. A
rule is written in SQL every engine reads, every caller property and
literal a bound parameter. The list pages with the condition, the read by
key selects by the key and the condition (outside the rows: not found),
and the create inserts in a transaction, selects the new row by its key
and the condition, and rolls back with 403 `<ENTITY>_OUTSIDE_ROWS` when it
is outside: one translation, read by the engine over the row as stored,
as WITH CHECK does, at the cost of an insert rolled back. A rule outside
the subset (a function, arithmetic, a comparison with no field) leaves
its entity unfiltered and keeps every warning, with the reason; an
operation whose body the service writes keeps its warning, now naming
`rows<Entity>`, and so does a job.

Cases: generate-go-dxlib-rows (the applied service; its implementation
file gains an sql target for the round trip) and
generate-go-dxlib-rows-warned, whose member rule now holds `size(title) >
0` so that its warnings stay the case of a rule nothing applies. A unit
test covers the translator. The round trip gains TestRowsRoundTrip: the
lending service with the migration generate sql writes, policies
included, on PostgreSQL 17.6 in a container, two organisations' loans,
and the generated client as each staff, a member, a member whose
memberId is null (a NULL parameter, so no row) and a caller holding no
role. It passes; with the handlers' filter switched off it fails at every
list, both cross-tenant reads and the create, which is G3.1b as found.
It runs when SPECARCH_DXLIB and SPECARCH_POSTGRES are set. SA-82 gains
the acceptance line; `docs/generators.md` says the design. No design
keyword changed and no dependency changed. Nothing was left out.

## Content pages: ordered blocks of headings, text, images and links

The third task of the order closing the blocking gaps (G1.2 and G1.8 of
the capability check). A page may now be of kind `content`, with
`blocks`: an ordered list in which each block is a `heading` (a level 1
to 6 and a text), a `text` (a paragraph), an `image` (a path and its
`alternativeText`) or a `link` (a `page` of the specification, or an
`href` outside it, with a `label`). An addition to meta-model 0.3
(ADR-181, SA-92, an acceptance line on SA-42); the `if` that requires
`blocks` also requires `kind`, since without it a page with no kind,
which extract writes, matched it.

A content page has no `entity`, `source`, `submit`, `fields`, `sections`,
`columns`, `filters` or `actions`; `blocks` on another kind, a route
parameter and a link to no page are refused (page), a link to a page
whose route takes a parameter too (flow). With an accessibility target, an
image without its text, a link without its label and a heading that
skips a level, the title being level 1, are refused (accessibility). A
flow's step takes a link by its label as event `action`. The derived
cases are those of a page that opens, so a page that is not public gets
`denied without <permission>` and, with a session, `denied with expired
session`. The model holds a link's page as a reference. Both builds print
the same.

The generators read the blocks through `genui.ContentBlocks`, which both
web generators share: specarch-gen-ui writes `<page>.html` alone, with no
module, and refuses a content page that needs a permission, since the
files reach anyone with the text inside; specarch-gen-ui-typescript
writes the schema and `ContentPage`, every text a string key under
`<page>.blocks.<n>` as a section's title is keyed by its place, inside the
`Guard` with the derived refusal test when it needs a permission, warned
that its text still reaches the browser; specarch-gen-ui-swiftui refuses
each content page by name and writes nothing for it. `ui-components`
1.9.0 adds the content-page part; the overrides in the cases and the
example move to 1.9.0. The techspec draws an edge per link and lists the
blocks; the manual says where each link leads and that a page is reached
from a link. The screens' new `ContentPage.tsx`, `schema.ts` and
`index.ts` reach every TypeScript case and both Next.js examples; the
generated content case and both examples pass tsc and eslint, and the
case's derived tests pass under node. The source `wcag-2-2` is added to
the specification's sources.

Cases: validate-page-content, validate-page-content-refused,
model-page-content, derive-page-content, generate-ui-content,
generate-ui-content-refused, generate-ui-typescript-content,
generate-ui-swiftui-content, document-techspec-content and
document-manual-content; validate-test-data's enum message lists content.
A public content page still draws the warning that it has no red
scenario, as a public task page does. Left out on purpose: assets as
files (G1.4) and the site output, `metaDescription`, `canonical`, a
sitemap and a static setting (G1.7), a follow-up. No dependency changed.
Found on the way and reported, not fixed: a flow step with no event
also gives an empty flow message at 1:1.

## Content pages, after review

The review of the content pages asked for five changes. The Next.js
generator wrote an image's path into `src` as it is, and a browser reads a
relative `src` against the page's own route, so on `/about/team` the path
`images/team.jpg` was asked for at `/about/images/team.jpg`; the screens'
schema now holds the path from the site's root, `/images/team.jpg`, and
generate-ui-typescript-content has a public content page on that nested
route. The plain JavaScript output writes every page at the site's root,
so its relative path already resolves there. The image path's pattern
now refuses a leading `/` (and so `//`, another host), a `.` or `..`
folder and a scheme, and both validators describe the pattern in plain
words. Two links of one label on a content page that lead to different
places are refused (`page`): a reader tells links apart by their labels
(WCAG 2.2, 2.4.4), and a flow step follows the first link of its label.
A page with blocks and no kind no longer reads "is a , and blocks are
what a content page shows": the schema's missing kind is the message.
The review also asked whether other keys of a form, a list or a task
(`onSubmitted`, `onSelect`, `childRows`, `compactColumns`, `inbox`,
`pickers`, `fieldConditions`, `checks`, `enteredTwice`, `states`) went
unread on a content page; each is already refused at its key by its own
check (`flow`, `picker`, `form_field`, `workflow`, `state`, `page`), and
`layout` is not a key of a page, refused by the schema, so no check was
added. validate-page-content-refused covers the paths, the repeated
label and the page with no kind; Go and Swift print the same.

## Content pages, second review

The second review found the image path's pattern still let a path leave
the site through a backslash: `\\other.example/x.jpg` matched, a browser
reads `\` as `/` in an http or https URL, and both web generators write
the path so that it resolves to `https://other.example/x.jpg` (Next.js as
`/\\other.example/x.jpg`, plain JavaScript as written). The pattern now
refuses `\`, `?`, `#`, `%` and white space in every folder too: a browser
drops a tab or a line break inside a URL, `?` and `#` end the path, and
`%` would let `%2e%2e` stand for `..`. The schema, its fragment copy, the
Swift copy and both validators' plain-word description changed together;
validate-page-content-refused gains a backslash path.

## Sign-in through an OpenID Connect provider

The fourth task of the closing-the-gaps plan (G2.1). The owner answered
Q3: OpenID Connect first, the authorisation code flow with PKCE, additive
to meta-model 0.3, SAML a later task. The owner also decided that the
identity token's `iss` and `aud` are the deployment's values, never fixed
in the design: `const` or `enum` on either is refused, and the callback
checks the deployment's `signIn.issuerUrl` and the client's `clientId`.
That is why `signIn` has a `client` key the plan did not list: the
callback needs to know whose client_id the token's audience is.

`signIn` (kind oidc, provider, client, flow authorizationCode, pkce
true, callback, identityToken, logout.backChannel) is checked in both
builds under the new rule `sign_in`. `navigate: signIn` is a reserved
word of the page event; a page's name is kebab-case, so the event's shape
did not change and no 0.8.0 question arose. It leaves the application
from a task page's answer or an action's `then`, with no `with` or
`message`. The callback derives six critical cases, `state does not
match`, `code used twice`, `signature invalid`, `issuer differs`,
`identity token expired` and `nonce replayed`, each with its security
event (`sign_in_*`) once `securityEvents` is declared, and none of the
token cases. A deployment gives `signIn.issuerUrl` (and `jwksUri`) and
the client's `clientId`. ADR-182 cites RFC 6749, RFC 7636, RFC 9700,
OpenID Connect Core 3.1.3.7, Discovery 4.3 and Back-Channel Logout 1.0;
SA-93 states it; the sources rfc-7636 and the three OpenID Connect
documents are added.

The generators: specarch-gen-ui writes `identity-provider.js` (named so
because a page `sign-in` already writes `sign-in.js`), which reads
`identity-provider.json` beside the pages, checks the discovery
document's issuer, keeps the state, nonce and PKCE verifier in the
`__Host-sign-in` cookie and sends the browser to the authorisation
endpoint; a task page's answer and an action's `then` load it.
specarch-gen-ui-typescript writes the provider into `application.ts`
from `NEXT_PUBLIC_SIGN_IN_ISSUER_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SIGN_IN_CLIENT_ID`,
and `signIn` in `service.ts` that TaskPage calls. specarch-gen-ui-swiftui
writes `Screens/SignIn.swift`, which signs in through the system's web
authentication session (iOS 17.4 or macOS 14.4, the service's host as an
associated domain) and calls the callback with the code and the state.
The go-dxlib handler of the callback stays `bodyFinishSignIn`. The
techspec's runtime view gains the sign-in section with a sequence
diagram and the checks table, the security events table the six events,
and the screen flow a node for the provider; the manual says the
provider's sign-in opens.

Cases: validate-sign-in-oidc, validate-sign-in-oidc-refused (every
reference of signIn, the identity token, the back-channel logout and the
event rules), validate-sign-in-oidc-deployment (a deployment with and
without the issuer and the clientId, in both builds), derive-sign-in-oidc, generate-ui-sign-in-oidc,
generate-ui-typescript-sign-in-oidc, generate-ui-swiftui-sign-in-oidc,
generate-go-dxlib-sign-in-oidc and document-techspec-sign-in-oidc, and a
unit test of the plain JavaScript action. The screens' changes reach every
UI case and the examples; the generated Next.js output of the case and
the Carbon example pass tsc, and the generated SwiftUI package and the
example's iOS package build on macOS (no simulator run: nothing here is
an app; the web authentication session itself is not exercised).

Found on the way and fixed, since this item needs it: BUG-059, a
deployment's `clients` were refused as a design key in both builds, so no
deployment could give a client_id.

Left out on purpose: SAML, sign-out beyond the back-channel logout
(G2.2), claims to roles (G2.6); an `audience differs` case on the
callback (the token's `aud` against the client_id), which OpenID Connect

## Sign-in through an OpenID Connect provider, review round 1

The review of the sign-in asked for two fixes and four notes; all are
taken.

- The dxlib dialect serves every operation as a POST at `/<operationId>`
  with a JSON body, and the provider sends the browser back to the
  callback with a GET and the code and the state in the query, so the
  sign-in could not complete there and nothing said so.
  specarch-gen-go-dxlib, specarch-gen-ui and specarch-gen-ui-typescript
  now warn at `/signIn/callback`, naming the GET route at the callback's
  path the service adds in front of the callback's body. A warning, not
  an error: the redirect and the handler are right, and the route is the
  project's to add, as other "apply it in the service" warnings are.
  SwiftUI refuses the dxlib dialect already.
- The SwiftUI sign-in threw away the status of `SecRandomCopyBytes`, so a
  failure would have sent 32 zero bytes as the state, the nonce and the
  PKCE verifier. It now refuses the sign-in, naming the `OSStatus`.
- The SwiftUI web authentication session now matches the redirect's own
  path, so a service URL with a path prefix (`https://host/api`) returns.
- Once the callback answers, the SwiftUI task page reads the session
  again and opens the page sign-in was asked for from, or the app's first
  page; before, the person stayed on the sign-in form with nothing said.
- The Next.js redirect is refused at run time, naming both origins, when
  the screens call a service on another origin than the page's, since the
  `__Host-sign-in` cookie would not reach the callback there. The plain
  JavaScript screens always call the page's own origin (`service.js`
  writes `service: ""`), so they need no check.
- `then: { navigate: signIn }` on an action in Next.js and SwiftUI: the
  first round left it refused there. The owner chose to write it now
  (answer to the review's question): a list's or a view's operation
  action carries the callback's path as `signIn`, and once the operation
  succeeds `ListPage` and `ViewPage` leave for the provider, through
  `signIn` of `service.ts` on Next.js and through
  `IdentityProvider.signInAndReturn` on SwiftUI, which the task page now
  shares; a sign-in that fails shows its reason on the page. Any other
  navigation after an action stays an error at the action's `then`.

Cases: generate-ui-sign-in-oidc-dxlib (new, the plain JavaScript warning),
generate-go-dxlib-sign-in-oidc (now with its warning), a unit test of the
Next.js warning in genscreens; generate-ui-typescript-sign-in-oidc and
generate-ui-swiftui-sign-in-oidc gain a view `case-view` whose action
Hand over leaves for the sign-in, with the session read from
`getSession` and the derived drafts of the new operations and page; every SwiftUI and Next.js golden, the hand-built copies and the
examples refreshed for the template changes. The case's Next.js output
and the Carbon example pass tsc; the case's SwiftUI package and the
example's iOS package build on macOS (simulator: deferred, nothing here
is an app).

## Custom pages: a screen the project builds by hand

Gap G3.7 of `docs/capability-check-2026-10-24.md`, the fifth task of
`docs/closing-the-gaps-2026-10-24.md`: an editor or a live view had no
page kind, so it had no route, guard, menu entry, flow or derived test in
the design. The page kind needed no owner's answer; an interface beyond
HTTP, such as a WebSocket the editor speaks, is the owner's question Q4
and is left out here.

- `kind: custom` on a page (ADR-183, SA-94, a line on SA-42): `route`,
  `title`, `permission`, an optional `entity` and `description`. Its
  route may take parameters, which the project's screen reads.
- Refused at the key (`page`): `source`, `submit`, `fields`, `sections`,
  `columns`, `filters` and `states`. The other keys that describe a
  generated screen (`compactColumns`, `blocks`, `childRows`,
  `onSubmitted`, `onSelect`, `pickers`, `fieldConditions`, `checks`,
  `enteredTwice`, `inbox`) are already refused on a page of the wrong kind
  by their own checks, so the new check leaves them to those and nothing
  is reported twice. An action of kind `operation` is refused; `navigate`
  actions stay, since a flow names the event that leads from one page to
  the next, so a flow passes through a custom page by an action's label.
  Both builds print the same.
- Derived cases: those of a page that opens, no "not found" case for a
  route parameter, since what the screen loads is the project's.
- The UI generators write the route, the guard and the menu entry, and
  import the screen from a file the project writes:
  `specarch-gen-ui-typescript` writes `page.tsx` drawing the default
  export of `./custom/<page>` beside it (as the item asked) with
  `CustomPageProps` from `screens/custom.ts`; `specarch-gen-ui-swiftui`
  writes `<Page>View` drawing `<Page>Screen`; `specarch-gen-ui` writes
  `<page>.html` with a notice that stays until the screen shows and
  `<page>.js` importing `custom/<page>.js`, named in `pages.js`. The plain
  JavaScript screens have no route guard for any page, so a custom page
  that needs a permission is a warning there, not a guard.
  ui-components 1.10.0 adds the `custom-page` part.
- The techspec lists a custom page as the project's own screen with its
  description; the manual says it is built by hand.

Cases: validate-page-custom, validate-page-custom-refused,
derive-page-custom, generate-ui-custom, generate-ui-custom-check (the
project's modules in the output folder, `--check` passes),
generate-ui-typescript-custom (with the project's `board.tsx` left as it
is), generate-ui-swiftui-custom, document-techspec-custom and
document-manual-custom; every Next.js and SwiftUI golden, the hand-built
