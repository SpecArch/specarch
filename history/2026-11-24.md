## The pdf draws the Mermaid diagrams

Summary: every pdf, a document's or a book's, now draws each Mermaid
diagram its Markdown carries instead of printing the source: the entity
diagram, each entity's states, each operation's and command's sequence,
the pages, each workflow, and the context, component, deployment and
network diagrams (ADR-250). The Markdown keeps its Mermaid blocks, the
html keeps drawing the entity diagram and the workflows, and a docx still
prints the source, its label now saying that the pdf draws it. The owner
had opened the generated documents and found the diagrams as source text
outside the html.

`internal/diagram` reads the part of Mermaid the document targets write
(flowchart LR and TD with their eleven node shapes, labelled and dashed
arrows and subgraphs; stateDiagram-v2; erDiagram with attributes and the
four cardinalities; sequenceDiagram with participants, messages and
notes) and lays each out with the Go fonts' metrics: a layered graph for
flowcharts, state and entity diagrams, laid out across or down, whichever
shows it larger, with each arrow's label on it, subgraphs framed, and a
crow's foot, a ring or a bar at each end of a relation; lifelines and
messages for a sequence. The pdf draws the result as a tagged Figure with
its bounding box and a description, its labels as real text, scaled to
the column and to one page; a heading followed at once by a diagram
keeps it on its page. A block it cannot read would be printed as its
source under a label naming the line and why; a test reads every Mermaid
block in the repository's Markdown, the design tests' expected outputs
and the examples' documents included, and draws each, so that label does
not show for anything SpecArch writes. The largest diagram, SpecArch's
own package graph, prints at 0.55 of its size; the others at 0.72 or
more.

ADR-248 had chosen to print the source because no Go renderer existed
and a second renderer would draw differently from Mermaid; ADR-250 rests
on it and on ADR-249 and keeps the Markdown's Mermaid for the viewers
that draw it. mermaid-cli was the other way: it needs Node.js and
Chromium and gives other bytes per version, so --check and approve could
not compare a pdf.

Two design tests: `document-diagrams-drawn-in-pdf` writes the techspec of
two related entities and a workflow as md and pdf, and
`document-html-draws-diagrams` writes its html; a Go test reads the three
outputs and requires the Mermaid blocks in the md, the drawn labels and
one figure per block and no Mermaid source in the pdf, and the SVG entity
diagram and the BPMN workflow with no Mermaid source in the html. The
book cases' expected pdfs and docx are recorded again. SA-149 gains the
acceptance line for the diagrams; `docs/document-formats.md` describes
the drawing.

The library lending example's techspec target lists `formats: [md,
pdf]`, so `examples/library-lending/docs/techspec.pdf` shows every
diagram drawn; the change to its implementation file moves the digest in
the header of every file generated from that specification. The handoff
mailbox was empty.

Left out on purpose: drawing the diagrams in the docx (Word needs a PNG
beside an SVG, or DrawingML shapes); it says where the drawing is.
