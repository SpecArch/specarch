origin, `gaps`, `approve` and the generate gate, and the design of tests
derived from a specification. The editor hints and the README's install

maintenance step is built: the validator reads the records kept beside a
specification and checks them against it.

## Derived cases ranked by harm and by mistakes

The first of the seven items in `docs/test-generation.md`. A requirement
may name its `harm` (data-loss, money, security, safety, privacy,
availability), and a field or a parameter's schema may say `mistakes:
frequent` or `mistakes: rare`. Every derived case has a frequency from the
table now printed in `docs/conventions.md`, and a rank: critical when its
subject satisfies a requirement with a harm or when it is a failing
dependency, frequent when its frequency is frequent, other otherwise.

`test_case_missing` now warns only for critical and frequent cases. On an
operation with no harm the boundary cases, the 4xx and 5xx responses and
the duplicates drop out of the warnings; a missing required field, a
caller without the permission, a pattern, an enum, a format, a not-found
lookup, a wrong state and a command's exit codes stay. The test plan has a
section "Derived cases left out" with every uncovered case of rank other,
its subject, its scenario and the reason. The traceability document and
chapter 13 of the techspec show a Harm column once a requirement names a
harm, and the requirements document names the harm of each requirement.
A field's `mistakes` shows among its limits in the techspec.

Specification first: SA-21 and SA-22 with NEED-9, ADR-020, ISO/IEC/IEEE
29119-4:2021 as a source, the rule text of `test_case_missing`, `harm` and
`mistakes` in the design schema. Both validator builds rank the same way
and print the same output; the documents are Go only. Six cases lost the
warnings they no longer get, `validate-derived-cases-listed` among them,
and five cases are new: harm making every case chosen, `mistakes` rare and
frequent with a failing channel, an unknown harm, the left-out section,
and the Harm column. In the example, LIB-3 names `harm: [money]`.

Decisions made here, for the owner to confirm or veto:

- A subject takes the harm of its own `satisfies` only. A constraint or a
  transition does not take the harm of its entity, so marking one does
  not mark the rest.
- Every derived case of a critical subject is critical, the golden
  boundary cases too, not only the red ones the design text first named.
- The Harm column appears only when some requirement names a harm, so a
  specification that does not use harm gets no empty column.
- The left-out table has a Scenario column where the design had Rank,
  since every row there has rank other.
- `mistakes` on a parameter's schema covers its `not found` case as well
  as its limits.

Open: the v0.2.0 schema, which the editor hints name, has neither `harm`
nor `mistakes`, so an editor flags both until the next release tag.

## What SpecArch takes from dxlib, and idioms

A design item, read-only in dxlib, dxlib_module and the two services built on
them. Two documents came out of it: `docs/dxlib-lessons.md`, the study the
owner asked for, and `docs/idioms.md`, the design of the idiom library the
owner decided on the same day. Nothing in dxlib was changed, and no code or
schema changed here; the README lists both documents and the roadmap
points at them from the SQL target and the 0.2 candidates.

The study starts, as the owner asked, from dxlib's unified type definition:
one struct rendered to the request validator, to JSON, to Go and to four SQL
dialects. The verdict is to adopt the model and not the encoding. The design
type stays SpecArch's structural one, since the keywords already say what
dxlib's fifty-five names say and cannot have the gaps a name registry has;
the rendering becomes a shipped, overridable table, the first idiom, keyed by
type, format, width and precision. The four-dialect SQL table is copied with
each engine divergence and the reason dxlib wrote beside it. Two findings
decided the home of the table: dxlib's own `JSONType` and `GoType` fields
are read by nothing, while the two renderings in use live where their
readers are; and the ERP backend has written two SpecArch-shaped YAML
languages of its own on top of dxlib, because the Go declarations were not
reviewable.

The map found the design side already holds entities, enums, operations,
permissions and state machines, and lacks what every dxlib service has:
field sensitivity and masking, encryption at rest, audit fields and soft
delete, one shape for list operations, background jobs, a standard error
body, limits, menus and a read model. Each is proposed as a 0.2 keyword with
its validator rule, and the error body takes RFC 9457 rather than dxlib's
shape, with the reason.

A Go implementation on dxlib is designed as a second Go route beside plain
Go: a `dialect: dxlib` setting on the OpenAPI target, since dxlib already
emits, reads and binds that document with drift fatal at start, and a
`specarch-gen-go-dxlib` for the model, table and handler declarations. Two
mismatches were read and are stated rather than wished away: dxlib's reader
refuses the JSON Schema bounds SpecArch writes, and dxlib routes by URI
alone. Both are dxlib items. The notification service is named as the real
project a go-dxlib generator would be accepted against; the umbrella
repository's focus rule leaves it waiting. Job C branches on whether the
private service is on dxlib, which could not be read from here.

Idioms are templates shipped in `idioms/`, embedded in the binary and
versioned with SpecArch; a project uses them by default, declares only
exclusions and overrides under `idioms` in its implementation file, and
keeps overrides as files beside it, each recording the shipped version it
was copied from. The lookup order is override, shipped rendering for the
stack, shipped stack-neutral part. The validator warns when a shipped
template is newer than an override's base. The first set of fifteen is
written from dxlib, each row saying what a tool checks and what is
guidance.

Decisions made here, for the owner to confirm or veto:

- An override may add contract statements and may not remove or weaken a
  shipped one; a different contract is an exclusion plus the project's own
  idiom under another name.
- A list on an engine without an array type renders as JSON text with the
  reason in the row, as dxlib does, rather than failing generation.
- A UUID column renders as the engine's native type where one exists and as
  36-character text elsewhere; a duration as an interval where one exists
  and as ISO 8601 text elsewhere.
- `format: time`, a time of day, joins the type list.
- An enum column is text plus a check constraint on all four engines, not
  the native enum types.

Left out on purpose: the names of a former private dependency and its owner
that a comment in dxlib_module mentions, the ticket and design references in
dxlib's comments, the product names in the operation ids of dxlib's OpenAPI
examples, and the country whose tax number dxlib validates. The handoff
mailbox was empty.

Open: whether the private service of job C runs on dxlib, which picks the Go
route; and whether the notification service's specification lives in its own
repository, which the focus rule holds for now.

## Records beside the specification

Step 2 of `docs/maintenance.md` is built in both validator builds. A
specification's records sit in `records/` beside its folder, the place
approvals already used: one file per change request, defect, release,
incident, commissioning run or approval, in a folder per kind. They have
their own schema, `schema/specarch-record-0.1.schema.json`, and eight new
rules: `record_name`, `record_ref`, `change_applied`, `change_decision`,
`defect_test`, `defect_duplicate`, `incident_link` (a warning) and
`commissioning_record`. Two new source kinds, `change-set` and
`defect-set`, let a record name an ID kept in an external tracker. A
`records/` folder inside the specification's own folder is a layout error
that says to move it up a level. SpecArch's own specification gains SA-23
for this. library-lending has a release, a commissioning run, an incident,
the defect it found and two change requests, one approved and one
rejected.

Four points the design left open are settled:

- A change's `affects` and a defect's `violates` hold a requirement ID or
  a `#/` pointer, nothing else. The design listed bare test, environment
  and setting names as well, but `production` could be an environment, a
  test and a setting at once, and the principles make a reference that can
  mean two things an error. A test is `#/tests/<name>`, and so on.
- A defect that turns out not to be a defect names the change request it
  became under `change`.
- An incident says whether the rollback was used in `rollbackUsed`, and
  names the role that reported it in `reportedBy` when no monitor caught
  it; the schema needs one of the two.
- Approvals, which `specarch approve` already writes, are a sixth kind of
  record and are checked like the rest, so the role that approved must be
  a stakeholder.

That last point turned up a fixture that could not happen: the case
`generate-with-plugin` held an approval by `owner` in a specification with
no stakeholders, which `approve` itself would refuse. The case now
declares the stakeholder, and its approval's digest was recomputed.

`record_ref` leaves three references to other rules so that none is
reported twice: a change's `affects` belong to `change_applied`, a
defect's `duplicateOf` to `defect_duplicate`, and a commissioning run's
check names to `commissioning_record`. A requirement in an external
requirement set counts as present and never as removed, since the
validator cannot read the set. A defect's test passes `defect_test` when
it verifies a requirement the defect violates, or when its subject is the
element a violated pointer points into.

The release rules (`release_contents`, `release_bump`, `release_version`)
are step 3 and are not checked yet; a release record is read for its
references only.

Twelve new cases, recorded from a run of the Go build: one valid tree with
every kind, the trackers, the schema, one per rule, and the layout error.
go vet, go test, swift test (13 pass), validate on `spec` and `examples`
in both builds and `document --check` for all seven targets pass. The two
builds print the same on all 160 case folders. No dependency change. The
handoff mailbox was empty.
