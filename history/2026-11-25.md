## Scenarios: one case as ordered steps across the design, checked and documented

Summary: a specification can hold scenarios under `scenarios`, keyed
`SC-n`: one case of the system, for one stakeholder and under one topic,
told as ordered steps that each name the operation, requirements, entity
change or tests they exercise, with alternative and failure branches as
steps under `when` (ADR-251 to ADR-254, SA-150). Both validator builds
check every name a scenario gives and warn of a must requirement no
scenario names; the requirements and technical specification documents
show the scenarios by topic with a sequence diagram, and the
traceability matrix traces requirements to scenarios and back.

The owner asked on 2026-11-25 for the sequence across requirements and
operations to live on the design side, not as free-form user stories on
the needs side, since those would repeat the needs and nothing would
check them. The decisions asked for are recorded: a scenario is for one
stakeholder and a step names another as its actor (ADR-252); its ID is
`SC-` and a number (ADR-253); extract lifts no scenario from feature files
yet, and derive drafts no test from a scenario yet, each a follow-up
(ADR-254).

- The keys are optional keys of the 1.0 schemas, as every addition since
  1.0.0 has been (ADR-233, ADR-244); the request asked for a new schema
  version, and no meta-model 1.1 is opened for one keyword (ADR-251). The
  fragment schemas and the Swift build's embedded schemas are written
  again from the design schema.
- validate: a scenario's stakeholder and a step's actor under
  `stakeholder`, a step's operation under `operation`, its requirements
  under `requirement` (the check `satisfies` and `verifies` get, now one
  function in each build), and under the new rule `scenario` a scenario
  with no step, a step naming nothing, a step's entity or test that does
  not exist and a test's `proves` naming no scenario. `scenario_uncovered`
  warns at a must requirement, neither rejected nor retired nor held
  elsewhere, that no step names, once the specification has a scenario;
  `info.scenarioCoverage: error` makes it an error, scenarios or none. A
  specification without scenarios gets no new warning.
- The documents: a Scenarios section in the requirements specification
  and a Scenarios part at the end of the techspec's runtime view, each by
  topic, each scenario with its stakeholder, steps table, sequence diagram
  and the tests that prove it; Requirements to scenarios, Scenarios to
  requirements and the uncovered must requirements among the gaps of the
  traceability matrix; a Scenarios group on the html page. The Indonesian
  catalog has every new text.
- `specarch model` lists the links of a step and a test's `proves`, in
  both builds.
- The library lending example has SC-1 (a late return and its fee, with
  the fee ledger's failure as a branch) and SC-2 (lending a copy, with the
  limit and an outstanding fee as branches), and `return-late`,
  `lend-a-copy` and `lend-limit-reached` prove them. LIB-1, LIB-2 and LIB-8
  are in no scenario, so the example shows three `scenario_uncovered`
  warnings.
- Cases: validate-scenarios, validate-scenario-problems,
  validate-scenario-uncovered, validate-scenario-coverage-error,
  validate-scenario-duplicate-id (SC-1 in two files is duplicate_key),
  document-requirements-scenarios, document-requirements-scenario-problems
  (every problem marked under the scenario it stands in, a step's under
  its scenario), document-traceability-scenarios and
  document-techspec-scenarios. The document command's definition names
  the new parts of the requirements, techspec and traceability targets. The two layout cases list `scenarios` among
  the design sections; the order of work (guide, AGENTS.md, the skill) has
  a step for scenarios.
- docs/conventions.md has the section Scenarios and the keyword row,
  docs/diagrams.md the scenario's sequence diagram, `help format` the
  section.

Left out on purpose: the request named no employer or client material, and
none went in. Open: whether to open meta-model 1.1.0 for keys like these
is the owner's call (ADR-251). The handoff mailbox was empty.

## Explain every option and its consequences before asking

Summary: a question carries what follows from each of its options under
`consequences`, keyed by the option's text (ADR-255, SA-151). `specarch
gaps --next` prints why the question is asked and, under each option,
its consequences or what to explain when none is written, and ends with
the rule: explain every option before naming any by its number. validate
warns, in both builds, of an option of a must or should decision question
that says nothing of what choosing it leads to, and reports an entry that
names no option as an error (`question_consequences`). The agent guide,
AGENTS.md, the skill and `specarch help asking` state the rule with a bad
example ("is A-1 ok?") and a good one from a library lending desk.

The owner decided on 2026-11-25 that an agent never asks a person to
approve an option by its ID alone: the message the person reads says what
the question is about and what it blocks, what each option means and
leads to (what changes, what it costs, what it makes easier or harder
later, what is hard to undo, who is affected), and the option
recommended and why.

- The key is optional in the 1.0 schemas, as every addition since 1.0.0
  has been (ADR-251). The request asked for a new patch version; a patch
  of the meta-model never changes what is valid, so the key ships in the
  program's next patch, 1.8.2, and no meta-model 1.0.1 or 1.1 is opened.
  A file on an earlier meta-model gets no new warning.
- Keyed by the option's text, not by position: an option reordered or
  renamed after its consequences were written shows a warning at the
  option and an error at the entry, instead of an explanation that
  silently describes another option. options keeps its type, so no reader
  and no --json field changes shape; `gaps --next --json` adds
  `consequences` beside `options`.
- The warning applies to every must and should decision question with
  options, as the owner asked, unlike the opt-in warnings of ADR-244 and
  ADR-251. Every existing question with options in SpecArch's own cases
  that is on 1.0 now shows it; the recorded outputs were read as diffs.
- `specarch merge` writes consequences for every question it asks with
  options (sources that disagree, one name for two elements, an element
  only the code or only the documents have, a grant only a seed makes,
  role names on the wire, a quantity the document and the check give
  differently), so a fresh merge does not trip its own warning.
- The questions document prints the written consequences under each
  option; the Indonesian catalog has the line.
- A new project gets the rule through `specarch skill --out`, which writes
  the skill holding it into the project's agent folder; `specarch init`
  still writes only the specification (ADR-210).
- The lending desk example's six questions with options have their
  consequences, Q-1 (the loan period) being the good example in the help
  text. The library lending example has no open question, so the
  example is taken from the lending desk, the same domain.
- Cases: validate-question-consequences, gaps-next-consequences and gaps-next-consequences-json; the
  gaps-next, merge, document problems and guide cases were recorded again.

Left out on purpose: the request named no employer or client material,
and none went in. The handoff mailbox was empty.
