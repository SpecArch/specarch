## Meta-model 0.3: the design and its steps, from the gaps handoff

The handoff `metamodel-gaps-from-sources.md` is processed in full.
`docs/meta-model-0.3.md` is the plan for meta-model 0.3: the caller as a
record an expression can name, and the three things that rest on it, the
rows a role reaches, machine clients with the gates on a route that are
not a permission, and token formats; what waits and why; and five steps,
each with what done looks like. ADR-094 to ADR-097 record the decisions. The roadmap's
section 5 and the 0.2 plan's left-out table point at the plan, and the
dxlib lessons name the rows rule as 0.3 step 1. Nothing is built: step
1 needs the schema, both validator builds, conformance cases and the
example, which is an item of its own, as the first 0.2 step was.

Where each of the handoff's eleven sections stands:

- built in 0.2: wire names (1, `info.wireNames`), a unique rule with a
  condition (2, `where`), approval workflows (4, `workflows`), pages not
  about an entity (5, task pages), an operation behind a setting (6,
  `enabledBy`) and a requirement's release (9, `release`);
- planned as 0.3 steps: gates that are not a permission (3), as `clients`
  with a proof per request after RFC 9421, `callers: clients` on an
  operation, and the reserved permission `nobody` for a route closed on
  purpose, step 3; token formats (11), as `tokens` with a lifetime, a
  signature algorithm, claims as properties under the one wire-name rule,
  and checks over the claims, each claim filling the caller property of
  its name, step 4; and the row-level rule the roadmap listed with them,
  as a role's `rows`, step 1;
- left out, with the reason in the plan's table: data in a cache and
  tables kept but unused (7), the implementation file's and `validity`;
  seed data per profile (8), which two extractions and `merge` compare
  now that the permissions reader is built, with a value that differs by
  environment being a deployment's setting; more than one deliverable per
  specification (10), a second implementation file.

What the reading settled: `caller.<property>` is the one field access an
expression may use, in CEL's own form, with `caller` reserved; that
reservation is the one change that refuses a valid 0.2 file, so the
version moves with step 1 rather than last, and a 0.2 file is read with
the version warning until 1.0. The rows rule sits on the role, after
PostgreSQL's row security policies granted to a role and combined with OR,
and a record outside the rows is not found rather than forbidden, after
the OWASP authorisation guidance. A client holds roles like a person, so
the fail-closed rule stays one rule, and `nobody` is told apart from a
permission forgotten in the roles by the word. Claims are properties
like any other, camelCase and on the wire as `info.wireNames` says, since
RFC 7519's registered names are single words and a name per claim would
be the second way to say a wire name that 0.2 refused. A property the
caller may lack allows null, and a comparison with it is typed as a bool
and false when null, which the expression checker and evaluator already
do for a field that allows null, so a rows rule is fail-closed without a
null test. `spec/specarch.yaml` gains the five sources the decisions cite (RFC 7519,
RFC 8725, RFC 9421, PostgreSQL's row security, the OWASP Authorization
Cheat Sheet).

Left out of the public files: anything naming the system the gaps were
found in, its tables, routes, clients or token names; the plan speaks of
an identity service, a lending library, a finance batch and an external
front end.

Checked: `validate spec examples` gives the same output in both builds (0
errors, 2 warnings, 7 open questions); go vet, go test and the fragment
schema check pass; the techspec and traceability documents are
regenerated and every `document --check` CI runs on `spec` and the
examples passes. No dependency change. Simulator: not needed, no app code.

Open questions for the owner, asked on the item, each with the
recommendation the plan takes: whether `caller.<property>` is the one
field access, with `caller` reserved and the version moved in step 1
(recommended), or flat prefixed names that break nothing; whether the
rows rule sits on the role with not found outside the rows (recommended)
or on the permission; and whether a machine route is `callers: clients`
with the clients holding roles, and a closed route the reserved
permission `nobody` (recommended), or clients named on the operation.

The owner accepted the three recommendations: `caller.<property>` is the
one field access, with `caller` reserved and the version moved in step 1;
the rows rule sits on the role, with not found outside the rows; and
machine routes are `callers: clients` with the clients holding roles, a
closed route the reserved permission `nobody`. ADR-094 to ADR-097 are
accepted. The plan was committed as 8fadd496; this commit records the
answers. Step 1 is left for its own item.
